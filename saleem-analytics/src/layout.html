The calendar and events steps use effects measured on past holidays and events, and an event in this period counts toward its own category’s average. By weekday

How to read this

Bars: the average day for each weekday in these weeks (dark), in the same number of weeks just before (light), and a year earlier (grey, same Hijri dates or same weekdays, as set under More settings). Averages rather than totals, so a weekday that happens to fall five times in a month doesn’t look bigger.

Table: vs before is the change in that weekday’s average against the weeks just before. Same move last year is the same change a year earlier; if both say −5%, nothing unusual happened to that weekday. Share of the week is how much of an average week that day carries.

Grid: one row per week, Saturday to Friday. The lightest cell is the quietest day in the grid and the darkest the busiest. Dotted cells are holidays. Read down a column to see whether a weekday is drifting, across a row to see an odd week.

How this is worked out

The gateway is the provider on a new patient’s first-ever real visit, as in the table above. A next service is any real service within the window after that visit: other services in the same first order count, and so does the first service coming back in a later order. A patient only counts once their window has passed, so recent months have fewer patients.

Each share is compared with all the other gateways of the same first service put together. With this many cells some gaps turn up by chance, so a cell is only marked worth checking when it passes the Benjamini–Hochberg test at 10% across every cell tested (20+ patients on both sides).

By first service

How this is worked out
  • The gateway is the provider on a patient’s first-ever real visit (started, finished or reviewed). Services with no provider, like most lab tests, show as No named provider.
  • Back within 30, 90 or 180 days: the patient had a real service in another order within that many days after the first visit. A patient only counts once that many days have passed.
  • vs typical: the provider’s 90-day return minus the median provider’s with the same first service, so one very large or very good provider doesn’t make everyone else look worse. With only one or two providers tested, they’re compared with the others together. Providers with fewer than 20 patients past 90 days aren’t tested. When providers differ from each other more than chance allows, the bar is raised to match.
  • Strong: passes the strict bar, 5% shared across every provider tested. Possible: passes the usual 5% bar for one test only, so with many providers a few turn up by chance. Treat them as leads.
  • A provider can look better because of the patients they get (area, age, reason for the visit), not only how they treat them.
  • The services and dates in the filter bar pick the first service and the cohort month. New patient means (under More filters) picks first visit or account created. Visit status and measure don’t apply here.

How this is worked out

Busy weekOther week

Service

How this is worked out

Full Saturday-to-Friday weeks in the date range. A provider’s share is their week divided by their whole service’s, unassigned included, so a week when everyone is busy doesn’t count as busy. A busy week is at or above the provider’s own 80th percentile, about 1 week in 5. Growth after a week compares the other service’s average over the next 2 (or 4) weeks with its average over that week and the 3 before. Busy weeks are compared with the provider’s other weeks (Welch t-test; the windows overlap, so each group counts as half or a quarter as many weeks). “Strong” passes a 5% bar corrected for every row and both horizons; “worth checking” passes a false-discovery check (Benjamini–Hochberg, 10%) across rows, so about 1 in 10 of those can still be chance. Each row shows the horizon with the smaller p. Shares within a service add up to 100%, so a colleague’s busy weeks can show the same result reversed. Timing that lines up is not proof that one causes the other.

AtoBto

Cases or doctors

How this is worked out

Everything is per day, so a 31-day month and a 28-day month compare fairly. The change in To is split into volume, what From’s own change gives if every From order led to To as often as in B, and rate, the change in To per 100 From.

The provider table uses the provider follow-on export when it’s loaded. For each doctor, nurse or physiotherapist under From, it shows the patients they saw and how many went on to a To service within 30 days, as a monthly average over each period’s months. It splits the change into seeing more or fewer patients (at B’s share) and a different share going on.

Cases or doctors? The follow-on to To is split three ways, by doctor specialty: cases (more or fewer patients overall, at B’s average share sent on), mix (the same number of patients spread differently across specialties, for example fewer internal medicine patients, who usually go on to lab tests), and doctors (a specialty sending a different share of its patients on). If the drop is mostly cases and mix, it came from the patients, not from the doctors ordering fewer tests. Cases a day come from the daily provider export when it’s loaded (exact dates); the share sent on comes from the provider follow-on export. When the latest month’s 30 days haven’t passed yet, it uses the 7-day window instead. Without that export, it shows each provider’s From orders a day from the provider export.

Does one lead the other?

Correlation of week-over-week changes in From and To, with To shifted later (positive weeks) or earlier.
How this is worked outA bar well above zero at +1 means a rise in From is usually followed by a rise in To a week later.
    How this is worked out

    Same-week links are often shared causes (holidays, ad pushes, salary days) rather than one service driving the other. A link that shows up one or two weeks later is the better sign of one service feeding another. Correlation is not proof of cause.

    Meta ads

    How this is worked out

    Each ad is matched to the service it promotes by its name: first its code (PT, NS, DRV, LT…), then words in the ad name, the ad set name, and finally the campaign name. Recruitment, app-install and awareness campaigns keep their own groups. Corrections go in ad_service_map.csv. Results are counted the way each campaign's objective defines them: WhatsApp conversations for message campaigns, purchases, installs or leads for the others. Cost per order divides spend by orders for the matching services in the same dates, so it is a rough efficiency signal, not attribution.
